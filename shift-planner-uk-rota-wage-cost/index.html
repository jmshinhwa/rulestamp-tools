What it checks, and the rule behind each check

  • Minimum wage by age: from 1 April 2026, £12.71 an hour for 21 and over, £10.85 for 18 to 20, £8.00 for under 18 and for the apprentice rate. From 1 April 2027: £13.21, £11.27, £8.31 and £8.31. The week's date picks the rate. Arrears = (minimum − pay rate) × paid hours. If you underpay, the arrears must be paid back at once, and HMRC can send a notice for the arrears plus a fine.
  • Daily rest: 11 hours between working days, 12 for under 18s. If someone finishes at 20:00 they should not start before 07:00 the next day.
  • Rest break: one uninterrupted 20-minute break when the working day is over 6 hours. Under 18s get 30 minutes when it is over 4.5 hours. A 20-minute gap in a split shift counts.
  • Weekly rest: 24 hours off each week, or 48 hours each fortnight. Under 18s get 48 hours a week.
  • Weekly hours: 48 hours a week on average, normally over 17 weeks, unless the worker opted out in writing. Under 18s: at most 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week, with no averaging.
  • Wage cost: paid hours × £ per hour. You can add holiday pay as a percentage, and employer National Insurance at 15% over £96 a week. Under 21s pay 0% up to £967 a week. Many small employers' Employment Allowance (£10,500 a year) covers their employer NI bill, so the NI box is off by default.

Sources read on 20 Nov 2026 on GOV.UK: National Minimum Wage and National Living Wage rates; Rest breaks at work; Maximum weekly working hours; The National Minimum Wage and Living Wage; Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027.
