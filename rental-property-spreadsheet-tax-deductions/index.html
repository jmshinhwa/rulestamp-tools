    What does this rental property spreadsheet work out?

    From 13 inputs (tax year, filing status, MAGI, your tax rate, active participation, loss carried in, your name, address, building basis, placed-in-service month, AFS, rents and the expense lines) it gives 6 results: whether the small taxpayer safe harbor fits, the amount deducted this year instead of over 27.5 years, the tax lost without the statements, Schedule E line 21, the loss allowed and the loss carried on.

    When can an improvement be deducted in one year?

    Under the small taxpayer safe harbor (Treas. Reg. § 1.263(a)-3(h)): average gross receipts of $10 million or less, building unadjusted basis of $1 million or less, and the year's total for repairs, maintenance, improvements and similar work on that building at or under the lesser of 2% of the basis or $10,000. You elect it each year with a statement attached to the return.

    What if the total goes over the cap?

    The cap is a condition on the whole year's total for the building, so the page treats the improvements as capitalized and depreciates them over 27.5 years from the month they were placed in service. In the example with a $360,000 basis the cap is $7,200, the total $7,475.00 is $275.00 over, and the $5,900.00 HVAC is capitalized.

    How is the passive loss limit counted?

    If you actively participate, up to $25,000 of rental loss can offset other income ($12,500 if married filing separately and living apart). It drops by 50% of MAGI over $100,000 ($50,000) and is gone at $150,000 ($75,000). The rest is suspended on Form 8582 and carried forward. The page assumes this rental is your only passive activity.
