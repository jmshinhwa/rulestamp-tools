How the balance check works

A bank prints a running balance after each transaction. Previous balance + deposit, or previous balance - withdrawal, must give the next printed balance, to the cent. The converter does that sum on every row. When a row does not add up, it shows what the balance column implies and how far off the amount is. That is the classic transposed digit that otherwise turns up at month-end reconciliation. In the example: $7,422.58 - $1,428.50 = $5,994.08, but the bank printed $6,174.08; $7,422.58 - $1,248.50 = $6,174.08, so the amount is $1,248.50, and the copied table adds up to $2,468.14 instead of the printed ending balance of $2,648.14. When a bank prints the balance only once a day, the rows of that day are checked together. On statements with no balance column, the total is checked against the ending balance.

What this version reads

PDFs that contain text, the kind you download from online banking. Amounts written like 1,234.56, with dates such as 10/02, 02/10/2026, 2026-10-02 or Oct 2. Scanned paper statements (images) are not read in this version: the page tells you when a PDF has no text. Password-protected PDFs ask for their password, which stays in this tab.

Questions

Is my bank statement uploaded anywhere?

No. The PDF is opened by code running in your browser tab, and the text never leaves your computer. Saved statements are kept in your browser's own storage on this device.

What does a red row mean?

The amount on that row plus the previous balance does not give the balance printed on the statement. Usually a digit was misread or the line was split. Open the text box, fix the line, and the row turns green when the sum is right.

Are there page limits or credits?

No. There are no page credits to run out.
