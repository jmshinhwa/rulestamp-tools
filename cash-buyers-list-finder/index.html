    How the list is built

    1. A sale counts as cash when the row has no loan amount (or 0), no lender and no financing word such as conventional, FHA or VA. Without any loan, lender or financing column the page says so and counts every sale.
    2. Deeds to banks, Fannie Mae, HUD and city or county agencies are dropped, and so are transfers under $1,000 (an owner moving a house between its own LLCs for $10 is not a purchase).
    3. One buyer = the same name once LLC, Inc, Co, punctuation and word order are ignored, or the same mailing address. Each merged row shows the spellings it joined and why.
    4. Rank = cash buys between the window start and the as-of date, then the most recent buy. Buyers whose last cash buy is older go to the stale line instead of your list.
    5. A buyer fits your deal when they bought cash in its zip inside the window, your price sits between 80% of their lowest and 120% of their highest cash price, and they bought that property type.

    Questions

    Where do I get the sold-records CSV?

    From any source that lists recorded sales with the buyer's name: your county recorder or assessor's sales search, a Propwire or PropStream export of sold properties, or an MLS sold export. Pull 12 months of sales for the zips you wholesale in. The page finds the columns by name and shows which ones it used.

    Why are 20 names only 9 buyers?

    County records type the same company in different ways: "MAPLE RIDGE HOMES LLC", "Maple Ridge Homes, L.L.C." and "MAPLE RIDGE HOMES L L C" are one buyer. In the example, RPG Holdings LLC is joined to Redline Property Group LLC because both use the same mailing address. Skip tracing each spelling would pay for the same investor twice.

    Does it give me phone numbers?

    No. It does not sell data or skip-trace. It turns the records you already have into a ranked list with one mailing address per real buyer, which is what you send to your skip-trace service or mail to.
