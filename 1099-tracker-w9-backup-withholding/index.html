Questions

What does this 1099 tracker work out?

From 7 inputs (tax year, your business, contractor list with W-9 dates, payment log, and the next payment's contractor, date and amount) it shows 5 results: backup withholding not held, what to hold back on the next payment, 1099-NEC forms needed, contractors with no W-9, and the Form 945 and 1099-NEC due date. In the made-up example: 1,536.00 dollars, 240.00 dollars, 4 of 5, 2, February 1, 2027.

When does backup withholding start on a contractor with no W-9?

With the payment that brings the contractor's total for the year to the line, and every payment after it. For tax years after 2025 the line is 2,000 dollars (IRS instructions, Rev. 12/2026). Treas. Reg. 31.3406(b)(3)-1 says the whole crossing payment is subject, not only the part above the line. Once a signed W-9 is on file, later payments are paid in full.

Why does the tracker ask whether I sent a 1099 last year?

The regulation drops the wait for the line when you were required to file a 1099 for that same payee for the preceding year. Then backup withholding applies from the first payment of the new year if there is still no TIN on file. In the example, Dev Patel's 2,200.00 dollars are all subject: 528.00 dollars instead of 312.00.

What happens if I did not hold back the 24%?

The IRS General Instructions say a payer who does not collect and pay over backup withholding may become liable for the uncollected amount. The tracker shows that amount per contractor and per payment. Backup withholding is reported on Form 945; for 2026 it is due February 1, 2027, or February 10 if every deposit was made on time.

I paid some contractors by card or PayPal. Do those count?

No. Payments by credit card, debit card or a payment app such as PayPal or Venmo are reported by the payment company on Form 1099-K, so the tracker leaves them out of the line and out of the 24% check. In the example, Eli Brooks was paid 2,500.00 dollars by PayPal and needs no 1099-NEC from the bakery.
